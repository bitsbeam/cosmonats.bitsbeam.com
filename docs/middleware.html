Middleware

#Server middleware

Every job execution on a worker runs through the server middleware chain. A middleware gets the job instance (nil when class can’t be loaded), its parsed payload, and the NATS message, and yields to run the rest of the chain:

Register middleware at boot, before workers start (e.g. in a Rails initializer; loading cosmo.yml afterwards keeps it). The chain starts with Cosmo’s required middleware: Cosmo::Middleware::Limit (a job class’s limit: option), Cosmo::Middleware::Totals (processed/failed counts, one per execution), and Cosmo::Middleware::Metrics (the Metrics tab). add appends innermost, after them; prepend, insert_before, insert_after, and remove reorder the chain, so moving or removing the built-ins is at your own risk. A middleware that raises sends the job through the usual retry/DLQ path, and one that doesn’t yield skips the job and acks it. To put a job back for later instead, raise Cosmo::Job::Requeue.new(delay_in_seconds): the job is redelivered without failing, though the redelivery still counts toward its retry and max_deliver. A fresh instance is built for every job. perform_sync doesn’t run the chain.

#Client middleware

The client chain runs where a job is enqueued (perform_async, perform_in, perform_at, perform_bulk, and the ActiveJob adapter), around the publish; for perform_bulk, yielding marks the job for its chunk’s publish. It starts empty, and a middleware gets the job class name, the payload Hash that will be published, and the stream the job runs on:

Not yielding stops the publish: perform_async returns nil, and a batch the job was joining doesn’t wait for it.
